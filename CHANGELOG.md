# Changelog

## [4.6.0] - 2026-09-11

### Added
- **A summary, chapters and transcript corrections, written by OpenAI with your own key.** A list glyph in the Player header (next to the share glyph) opens a card for the episode: a summary of three or four sentences, the episode's chapters — a story, a guest, a book, an advertising break — each with its start time and a one-line blurb, and the corrections the model proposes for the transcript. Tap a chapter to play from there; the one being heard is marked as the audio moves. The first time, the card names the model and what the run should cost, then a button sends the transcript — one timed sentence per line, with the episode notes for the spelling of names — to OpenAI's Responses API and writes the answer into the database (`EpisodeChapters`, `Episodes.summary`, schema v11). The key and the model live in Settings → **Episode assistant**: *Luna* (`gpt-5.6-luna`, the budget tier, about a cent for an hour of audio with the corrections, under a cent without) is the default, *Terra* (`gpt-5.6-terra`, eight cents or so) the alternative, and a switch runs the pass after every transcription instead of on request. Nothing is sent without a key typed there; a ChatGPT subscription does not include one (`api/openai.js`, `services/aiService.js`, `components/transcript/ChapterSheet.js`).
- **Transcript corrections, on trial.** With *Also correct the transcript* on (the default), a second request asks the model for the places where the recogniser wrote the wrong words — a misheard name, a title, a homophone — with the heard text copied verbatim and the sentence it sits in. A correction whose heard text is not in the transcript is dropped; a medium-confidence change to an ordinary word is kept but not applied (the card shows it greyed as *held back*); the rest are written into the text you read the way the names pass writes its own (`EpisodeFixes`, applied on read after `EpisodeNames`; the recogniser's words stay in `Transcripts`). The card lists every one — heard → correct, kind, confidence, how often, where — and tapping one plays from where it was said, so the trial can be judged: real fixes against wrong ones. Every run is written to the Debug Log with its chapters, fixes, tokens and cost.
- **Slide a sentence to the right to translate it.** The long-press still works, but holding a finger still for half a second on every sentence felt slow (user: "sometimes is slow to take a complete translation of sentence"). A thumb slid left to right across a sentence now opens the same translation card: the words follow the thumb a little, a globe appears in the left margin, and letting go past about a centimetre and a half — or a quick flick — opens it. A vertical drag is still a scroll, a tap still seeks or opens a word, and the gesture uses the same handler as the long-press, so the notebook pencil and pause-while-looking-up behave exactly as before (`components/TranscriptHighlighter.js`, `SWIPE_*`).
- **A translation you don't trust can be read again with the lines before it.** The free engine still answers every long-press and slide, instantly and at no cost, and it is what you get by default. When its answer reads wrong — a pronoun with no antecedent, a one-word reply, a joke that only the previous line sets up — *Translate with the lines before* under the translation hands the paragraph to the model instead, with what was said just before it as context it may read but not translate. The new translation replaces the old one and is marked; the English, the word taps and the notebook note stay exactly as they were, and a paragraph read this way keeps that reading while the app is open. It costs about a tenth of a cent and takes a second or two, so it is a button rather than a setting, and it only appears once there is an API key in Settings → Episode assistant. A failure leaves the free translation on screen and says why (`components/transcript/TranslationModal.js`, `services/aiService.js` `translateParagraphs`).

### Fixed
- **A long sentence no longer breaks off mid-phrase and carries on in the next paragraph.** A paragraph is the unit you tap, slide to translate and keep in the notebook, so one runaway sentence must not become all of them — but the old limit cut at a fixed thirty-five words wherever that landed, which was usually mid-phrase: one paragraph ended "…so that you don't have to wade through the" and the next opened "chaff that we are paid and deployed to do." The limit is now fifty words, and when a sentence does run past it the cut lands on the last comma, semicolon, colon, dash or bracket in the tail, where a reader pauses; only a sentence with no such boundary in its last twenty words is still cut at the limit. Measured on a 28-minute episode: seven mid-phrase breaks became three, all three on a comma (`components/TranscriptHighlighter.js` `CHUNK_MAX_WORDS`, `clauseCut`). A note kept in the Notebook on the *second* half of one of those old broken paragraphs will no longer find its sentence, since that half now starts at a different word.
- **A card with no buttons along the bottom no longer hides its last line behind the system navigation bar.** The bottom clearance belonged to the footer, and every card had one until the summary card arrived without — leaving its *Summarise this episode* button under the navigation buttons, where it could not be pressed. The clearance now goes to whichever is last, the footer or the body, and no existing card moves (`components/transcript/SheetModal.js`).

## [4.5.1] - 2026-09-10

### Added
- **Every episode a podcast ever published, from one button.** A podcast in My Podcasts unfolds to its latest five; a **More episodes** row under them now opens the whole back catalogue. The feed is fetched once and read a hundred items at a time (`api/rssParser.js` `openFeedHistory`: the document is cut down to one page of `<item>` blocks before the parser sees it), so a 3,000-episode show like *The Daily* lists its first page in about a second and fills in behind while you scroll or search by title — without the one long parse that froze the app and made the Feed cut every list at 50. Episodes already on the device show with their downloaded / transcript / played marks and position; the rest stream or download like any other row and are written to the database only when you open or download one, as already seen — an old episode you went looking for is not news, so it never counts towards the badges. Offline, the screen shows the episodes on the device and loads the rest when the connection returns; a feed that fails to load says so, with a Retry. A YouTube channel gets the same row once it holds more than five videos, since the accordion showed only five of them. Two things made this safe: an episode you started or finished from the back catalogue is no longer deleted when My Podcasts trims a feed to its latest 50 (only downloads and transcripts were kept), and a download that finishes after such a trim re-creates its row instead of leaving an orphaned file.

### Changed
- **A live-radio session paused for 30 minutes ends on its own.** Pause was open-ended: the recorder kept writing segments and, with a transcript, the speech engine kept decoding every 24 seconds for a listener who had moved on. Now a pause of up to 30 minutes is still just a pause (press play and carry on where you stopped, or catch up with LIVE); past that the session stops as if you had pressed Stop, and the station card says so while it is paused. The clock is the player's own Paused / Stopped state for the session's track, checked on every state change and on the minute poll as well as by a timer, so a background-throttled timer cannot stretch it (`services/radioService.js` `PAUSED_STOP_MS`).

### Fixed
- **The "Episode finished" prompt no longer vanishes when you skip to the end with +10.** It was appearing — and the very next tap of the skip button, landing on the dark backdrop around the card, dismissed it as "Keep" before it could be read (reproduced on the emulator: nine taps show the card, the tenth removes it). That prompt is now answered by its buttons only (the Android back button still means Keep), and every alert ignores a backdrop tap in the first 0.7 seconds after it appears, so a tap already on its way never counts as an answer (`components/AppAlert.js` `dismissible`, `BACKDROP_GRACE_MS`).
- **A long-press on a sentence works right after scrolling the transcript, and taps no longer die until the next scroll.** React Native's list takes every touch for itself while it believes a fling is still slowing down; it learns that from a native "momentum begin" event that Android sends at every drag release, and forgets it only on the matching "momentum end", which Android drops when a finger lands before the list has been still for three frames. So the natural gesture — scroll to a sentence, hold it — was swallowed, and the list then stayed convinced it was moving: every later tap and long-press on a sentence did nothing until another scroll happened to deliver an end event (user: "I need to move the scroll to work"). The transcript list now switches that check off on Android, where the native scroll view already intercepts touches during a real fling, so nothing is lost (`components/TranscriptHighlighter.js` `disableAnimatingCapture`).

## [4.5.0] - 2026-09-09

### Added
- **People's names are spelled the way the episode's own notes spell them.** The recognizer hears a name it has never seen and writes what it hears — "Chetin Inanch", "Björger Lililian", "Nissy Schaul", "Lisa Yassik" — and no on-device model can do better: its vocabulary has no Turkish İ, and it never emits ø or ç for English speech. The show notes, the episode title and the podcast's author field usually spell those same people right (on *The History Hour*, nine of the twelve misspelled names were in the notes). `services/nameText.js` takes the people named there as candidates — roles stripped ("Actor Peter Coyote"), shows and places dropped ("Turkish Star Wars", "Georgia Tech") — finds the capitalised runs in the transcript that sound like them (spelling with diacritics folded, plus a phonetic key with vowels and doubled letters removed, weighted towards the surname), and records one correction per heard spelling. A name confirmed once propagates: its heard surname and first name are fixed wherever they recur alone ("Inanch" fourteen times), and near misses of them mid-sentence ("Inanc", "Ura" for Uhura). The corrections go into `EpisodeNames` (schema v9) and are written into the text when a transcript is read — the Player, the export and the book scan, which now sees the author as the notes spell her — while `Transcripts` keeps the recognizer's words. The pass is offline and takes about a tenth of a second for an hour of speech, so it runs when a transcription finishes, when the Player opens a transcript from before this existed, and at launch for the whole backlog (`services/nameIndex.js`). Measured on the History Hour episode: 26 corrections, all right, covering İnanç, Lillelien, Shawl, Yaszek, Nichelle Nichols, Swearingen and Uhura; names the notes do not mention (Nimoy, Barrymore, Thatcher) are left as heard. The name extractor also now accepts capitals beyond A–Z, so "Çetin İnanç" and "Éric Vuillard" count as names in the book scan too.
- **Share the transcript.** A share glyph in the Player header (where a radio station shows its stop button) hands the whole transcript to the system share sheet as text: one line per sentence, led by the time it starts (`[12:34] …`, with hours when needed), the show and episode title on top. For keeping it, searching it, sending it to an assistant — and for laying it next to another transcript of the same episode to see where the recognizer went wrong, which is how the two fixes below were found (`components/transcript/share.js` `buildTranscriptExport`).
- **A notebook for the ideas, next to the vocabulary for the words.** Long-press a sentence and the card that translates it now has a pencil beside copy and share: tap it and the sentence is kept, and a note field opens under the English text for what it meant to you — the main idea, why it matters, how you would put it. The note saves as you type and the sentence is remembered by its place in the episode, so long-pressing it again reopens the same note (the pencil shows as a check; tapping it again removes the sentence, after asking if there is a note to lose). Settings → Learning → *Notebook* lists everything kept, filed under its show and episode in the order it was said, so an episode's notes read like a summary of it: tap a sentence to change its note or to play the episode from just before it, swipe left to remove it, search across sentences, notes and titles, and share the whole notebook — or the search's matches — as text, one timed quote per line with its note beneath. Notes outlive the episode's audio and transcript, and the episode row itself (`Notebook` table, schema v10; `services/notebookService.js`, `screens/NotebookScreen.js`). Typing in the sentence card lifts it above the keyboard (`SheetModal`), which the edge-to-edge window on Android otherwise leaves covering the field.

### Changed
- **A finished episode leaves the Library.** The Library listed every download, so an episode heard to the end sat in its podcast folder next to the ones still to hear (user: "if an episode is completely played it should not appear in the Library"). It now shows only downloads not yet finished; a finished episode stays in Listening → *Finished* until its file goes, and replaying it — or *Unplayed* in Listening — brings it back to the Library. Folder counts follow.

### Fixed
- **A word no longer comes back twice where two transcription windows meet.** A file is decoded in 29-second windows, and they were cut at a fixed sample count, so about as often as not the cut fell inside a word; the recognizer heard half of it in each window and returned it from both sides — "wherever you get your." / "your BBC podcasts.", "however." / "However, as…", eight times in one hour of *The History Hour* — or truncated ("wide." for "widely quoted"). The native reader now moves the end of every full window to the quietest 200 ms of its last four seconds (summed energy over 10 ms hops, never leaving a window shorter than 20 s) and hands the cut-off tail back to the decoder stream, so windows stay gapless and the cut lands in a pause (`ASRHandler.seamCutIndex`, `AudioWindowReader.pushBack` in the sherpa-onnx.rn patch). When speech runs through the whole search span and a cut still falls inside a word, the JS side drops the second copy — the same word ignoring case and punctuation, the first ending at the seam and the second starting there (`whisperService` `dropSeamDuplicate`); a real repeat ("had had") is spoken away from a seam.
- **A book whose title is an ordinary word is found when its author was said.** "My next read is Enough by Dawn French" (*The Radio 2 Book Club*) came out as the right candidate — title *Enough*, author Dawn French — and was then lost in the catalogues: Open Library has 6,551 works called Enough and the first page of twenty, like Goodreads' five autocomplete hits, holds none of hers, so both matchers came back empty and the mention stayed plain. When the title alone matches nothing and the transcript named the author, the resolver now asks once more for that writer's book — Open Library by title *and* author, Goodreads with the author's name added to the query — and the same thresholds decide, so nothing gets in that the first round would have refused (`services/bookResolve.js`, `api/openLibrary.js`). Two more paced requests, only for a candidate with an author that failed. Episodes scanned before this build are scanned again at launch.
- **"E.T." is no longer a sentence of its own.** The reader split a chunk at any word ending in `.` `?` `!`, so every "E.T." — and "Mr.", "U.S.", "etc." — closed a sentence. `services/sentenceBoundary.js` now decides: a title (Mr., Dr., St.…) never closes one, an initialism or abbreviation (E.T., U.S., etc., No.) closes it only when a capitalised word follows, and a lowercase next word means the stop was inside the sentence. The transcript reader, the export and the no-timestamps window fallback share the rule.

### Notes
- Measured on *The History Hour* — "E.T. the Extra Terrestrial and the creator of Star Wars toys" (Parakeet 0.6B) against Apple Podcasts' transcript of the same episode: the two agree on 94 % of the words; where they differ Apple is right far more often, overwhelmingly on names (İnanç, Lillelien, Nisi Shawl, Nimoy, Thatcher…). Podink's transcript also lacked about 230 words in twelve passages that the same model on a Mac transcribes in every window alignment tried, so the cause is on the phone and still open — the export exists to find it.

## [4.2.0] - 2026-09-07

### Added
- **Books mentioned in an episode are set in bold in the transcript, and tapping one opens a book card.** After a transcription finishes (and once for episodes transcribed before this existed, when the Player opens them online), `services/bookIndex.js` scans the text for the ways people name a book — *The Gilt Kid* **by** *James Curtis*, "her novel is **called** Permafrost", "*Alan Moore's* The Great When" (`services/bookText.js`) — and checks every guess against **Open Library** first and **Goodreads** second (`services/bookResolve.js`, `api/openLibrary.js`, `api/goodreads.js`): the catalogue is searched by the *title*, and the hit whose author sounds like what was heard wins, so the recognizer's "Ava Balthazar", "Susan Choy" or "Ruth Praoab Vala" come back as Eva Baltasar, Susan Choi and Ruth Prawer Jhabvala. A title said without its author is kept only when it is unmistakable or the author is one the episode already confirmed. The confirmed books go into `EpisodeBooks` (schema v8, one row per book per episode, with the spellings the transcript used) and every mention — in any of those spellings — is **bold** in the Player, in the sentence view and in the word-by-word view alike; a one-word title only where the transcript capitalised it. Tapping a bold title opens the **book card** (`components/transcript/BookSheet.js`, laid out like the word card's Wikipedia block): cover, title, author, rating with its count, year and pages, the description (folded with *Show more*), the spelling it was heard under when that differs, and buttons for **Open Library** and **Goodreads** (the book's own page, or a search for it when one catalogue had nothing) plus **Replay** from that mention; playback pauses while the card is open, as for a word. Lookups are paced (Open Library resets connections above about one request a second) and run in the background; a scan while offline is postponed to the next time the Player opens the episode. Deleting a transcript deletes its books. Measured on a 62-minute *Backlisted* episode with the fast Parakeet model: all six featured books and most passing mentions found, none invented.
- Scans run **one at a time through a single queue** (the lookups are paced to what Open Library tolerates, and two scans side by side would lose books to refused requests): the episode on screen and a transcript that just finished go to the front, and **at launch every transcribed episode that was never scanned** — everything transcribed before this build, or scanned while offline — is queued behind them, most recently listened first. In **Listening**, a row whose transcript names books shows a small **book badge with the count** next to the green download and transcript marks.
- **Interviews with the author in the room** name the book without its author — "your new book, Taipei Story", "this novel" — and the first phone test (The Waterstones Podcast, *Rebecca F. Kuang returns!*) found nothing. The scan now also takes **"book / novel / memoir / debut… + Title"** as a candidate, and it reads the **episode title and show notes**: the names in them (the interviewed writer, a collection's author) are hints the lookup may match a no-author title against, so *Taipei Story* resolves to R. F. Kuang's 2026 novel, and books the notes name — "author of Babel and Yellowface", "The Dragon Republic (2019)" — are looked up too and placed where the transcript first says them (a book the notes name but nobody says stays unbold and uncounted). Episodes scanned by the earlier detector are scanned again at launch.
- **The author's other books.** "We had a lovely chat back in Yellowface days" names a book with no cue at all. For every writer the episode points at — named in its title or notes, or confirmed by a mention — the scan pulls her works from Open Library and looks for each title in the transcript, whatever way the recognizer split it ("Yellow Face", "Palmhouse"); a title said that way is looked up under the catalogue's spelling and marked under the transcript's. Bold alone was hard to see in the dimmed text of both themes, so a book title now sits on a **purple highlighter band** (the palette's purple at low alpha) in the primary text colour, in the sentence view and the word-by-word view alike, whether spoken or not — the user's pick over bold or underlining.

### Changed
- **Player header tint is less grey on the dark theme.** The artwork colour that fills the Player header was softened so hard on the dark theme (saturation capped at 0.45, lightness pinned to 0.16–0.30) that a yellow cover became grey-olive. The cap is now 0.65 with a slightly lighter band, so the header keeps the cover's hue — gold rather than olive, a real blue rather than slate — while staying a calm surface for the title. The paper theme's tint is unchanged.

### Fixed
- **The transcript no longer drifts out of sync after a seek** (user: "if you seek the current podcast, at some point it desynchronizes; when you return to the zone that was in sync it is out of sync too; you can sync again if you start at the beginning, but it desyncs again at some point"). The highlight itself was right — a binary search over word times against the position the player reports — but on Android that position was wrong after every seek. ExoPlayer seeks an MP3 through the Xing/Info table of contents most encoders write (LAME, ffmpeg): 100 entries at 8-bit precision, so a seek lands up to 1/512 of the file away from where it was asked to — on *Rebecca F. Kuang returns!* (25 min, plain 128 kbps CBR with an ffmpeg `Info` frame) the audio comes back +3.7 s off at 1:00, +2.6 s at 2:00, +2.4 s at 25:00 — while the player reports the exact time requested, so from then on every word was highlighted seconds early or late until the next seek moved the error again; only a seek to 0:00 is exact, which is why starting over "fixed" it. Every seek did this: the resume on opening an episode, ±10 s, the slider, tapping a sentence, *Replay*. The fix is in the player, not the transcript: react-native-track-player drives ExoPlayer 2.19 through KotlinAudio, which it pulls as a prebuilt AAR, so **KotlinAudio v2.1.0 is now built from source** (`android/kotlinaudio/`, substituted for the JitPack artifact in `android/build.gradle`, one change documented in its README) and audio read from local storage — downloaded episodes, imported audio, YouTube imports — opens its MP3s with ExoPlayer's **index seeking** (`LocalFileExtractorsFactory.kt`): an exact byte↔time index built as the file is read, so a seek lands on the sample asked for; a far forward seek into a part not yet indexed scans the frame headers in between first (disk reads only, no decoding — well under a second for an episode). A file with no Xing/Info/VBRI frame keeps a constant-bitrate duration estimate until the exact length is known at the end, so its seek bar and *finished* detection still work. Streams keep the old seeking (index seeking would download everything up to a far seek target) and have no transcript anyway. Android only; M4A / Opus files were already exact.

## [4.1.0] - 2026-09-07

### Added
- **Import a YouTube video as an episode with a transcript.** Paste a video link — `youtube.com/watch?v=…`, `youtu.be/…`, a Short, a live replay, the m. / music. hosts, or a whole shared message with the link inside — into the new *Import from YouTube* screen (My Podcasts → folder button → *From a YouTube link…*, or the Feed's add box, which now recognises a video link and hands it over), or **share the video from the YouTube app and choose Podink**: the app is a share target for text (`ShareIntentModule.kt`), and the import starts on its own. On the device, NewPipe Extractor (`YouTubeModule.kt`, GPL-3.0, v0.26.5 from JitPack — the same library the NewPipe app uses, so it follows YouTube's changes) reads the video page and its audio-only streams; the best one is downloaded — the original-language track, M4A (AAC) before WebM (Opus), the higher bitrate first — in 8 MB range chunks (a single long request is throttled to real time), retrying a dropped chunk from its offset and re-resolving the stream URLs once if the media server refuses them. The audio lands in `youtube/<videoId>.m4a` and becomes an episode of the **channel** — a My Podcasts row of kind `youtube` with the channel's name and avatar, expandable like a feed — with the video's title, length, and notes that open with *Watch on YouTube*, the upload date and the view count above the video's own description. It is filed as downloaded, tops the Feed like a new episode, appears in Library and in Listening's *Downloaded*, and its **transcription is queued straight away**, exactly as after a podcast download; the import screen follows every step (reading the page, download progress with size and format, saving, then the transcription's own progress) and ends with *Open in Player*. The screen's state is the service's (`services/youtubeService.js`), so it can be left and re-opened mid-import; one import runs at a time. Errors are named: live streams (only finished videos), age-restricted, private, paid or geo-blocked videos, an unavailable link, a CAPTCHA demand, a network failure, a refused download, and an extractor that no longer understands the page (an app update will be needed) — each with the underlying reason.
- Imported videos behave like imported audio where it matters (`episodeService.isImportedEpisode`): deleting one (Library / Listening swipe) deletes the episode for good — nothing could stream it again — and a channel left without videos goes with its last one; the weekly clean-up of finished downloads and the end-of-episode *Keep / Delete* prompt leave them alone; *Remove channel* on the My Podcasts row deletes its videos, audio and transcripts (saved vocabulary stays). Importing a link again after deleting refreshes the file while keeping the listening state. The Feed refresh skips channel rows (no feed to fetch), and the launch-time orphan sweep also clears `youtube/` files (and `.part` downloads) no row refers to.
- Build: core-library desugaring (`desugar_jdk_libs_nio`) is on, which the extractor needs below API 33; the APK grows by the extractor, Rhino and jsoup. Android only.

### Fixed
- **A transcript that stopped part-way can be continued from the Player.** When a transcription was cancelled from a list, failed after a few windows, or the app was killed mid-way, the Player showed the text saved so far and nothing else — the *Transcribe episode* button lives on the no-transcript card, which is not shown once there is any text, so only the row's Transcribe pill in the Feed or Library could restart it (user: "when it doesn't have the transcription I can't tap inside to transcribe like a normal podcast"). The Player now shows **Transcript incomplete · Continue** over the text (the same badge that reads *Transcribing… 42 %* while a job runs, and *Queued for transcription* while it waits); tapping it re-queues the episode, which resumes from where it stopped. Applies to podcasts, imported chapters and YouTube videos alike.

## [4.0.0] - 2026-09-06

### Added
- **Live Radio** — a new tab with eight English-language talk stations, each shown with its logo (`assets/radio/`): 🇬🇧 BBC Radio 4, 🇦🇺 ABC Radio National, 🇮🇪 RTÉ Radio 1, 🇨🇦 CBC Radio One, 🇬🇧 BBC Radio 4 Extra, 🇳🇿 RNZ National, 🌍 BBC World Service and 🇺🇸 NPR (`services/radioStations.js`). A station's page shows what is **on air now** (title, description, time span with a progress bar) and the **next two programmes**, read from the broadcaster's guide (`services/radioSchedule.js`: BBC RMS, ABC Radio programitems, the CBC programme guide page, the RNZ schedule page; RTÉ and NPR publish nothing the app can read, so their stream's own "now playing" title stands in when there is one). Then two ways to listen:
  - **Listen live** plays the stream itself. The Player shows the programme guide instead of a transcript, the controls read LIVE, and the skips are off — a live stream has no past.
  - **With transcript** records the stream and transcribes it on the device as it plays (`android/…/LiveRadioModule.kt` + `services/radioService.js`). The recorder pulls the station — an HLS live playlist (BBC, ABC: MPEG-TS demuxed to ADTS) or an Icecast MP3 / AAC stream (RTÉ, CBC, RNZ, NPR; ICY metadata stripped) — parses every frame and rewrites it as a local HLS *event* playlist of 6-second packed-audio segments whose lengths are exact frame sums, so the player can seek through everything recorded while the file keeps growing. Every four segments (~24 s) go to the speech engine as one window; the words land in the session's transcript with their offset and the Player follows them like any podcast: rewind, skip, replay a sentence, word cards, translation, vocabulary. Playback starts when the first window has text — about half a minute behind the air — and a **LIVE** button in the time row (showing how far behind you are) jumps to the newest transcribed moment. The episode transcription queue pauses while a radio session transcribes (the running job is cancelled with its resume marker and re-queued afterwards) so the decoder is free for the live windows.
  - Later the same day, on the user's notes from the phone: **Coming up** rows carry the programme's description and length, not just a subtitle; the Player's live indicator was rebuilt around the *follow position* — playback keeps the delay it started with (≈ 25–30 s: one window plus its decode), so the pill reads **LIVE** while following and otherwise how far behind that point you are (tap to catch up), a caption shows the delay behind the broadcast, and the slider and skip-forward stop at the text edge instead of running into audio nobody can read yet; live transcript rebuilds no longer wait for "interactions" to end (a scroll counts as one), so new words no longer appear all at once after the finger lifts, with the bottom of the list looking empty until then. **Six more stations**: 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁳󠁣󠁴󠁿 BBC Radio Scotland, 🇬🇧 BBC Radio Ulster, 🇬🇧 LBC, 🇺🇸 WNYC (guide from its whats_on API), 🇦🇺 ABC NewsRadio and, asked for by name, 🇬🇧 BBC Radio 5 Live (sport commentary is often blanked outside the UK).
  - Then, from the phone again ("takes too much time to refresh the subtitle and I can't read the next text — could the delay be 40 seconds?"): playback used to start the moment the first window had text, about 27 s behind the air, which left nothing to read ahead just before each new 24 s window landed. It now waits until **40 s** are buffered (`FOLLOW_DELAY_SEC`), so between 15 and 35 s of upcoming words are always on screen; LIVE, the delay caption and Go-live follow the same figure.
  - 2026-09-07, from the phone again: **every station now has a programme guide.** RTÉ Radio 1 reads the JSON behind rte.ie's live-stations strip — the programme on air with its description and Dublin times; RTÉ publishes no day schedule the app can reach, so the card says so and has no *Coming up*. LBC reads its schedule page, whose Astro island carries the whole week as JSON with exact times. NPR publishes no timetable for its Program Stream at all, so the stream's regular weekly line-up (as PublicRadioFan lists it, Eastern time, with a line about each programme) is built in and labelled as such under the guide — NPR may interrupt it for news, and the hours the line-up leaves out stay blank. **Live radio without a transcript can no longer be seeked from anywhere.** The Player already hid the slider and greyed the skips, but the notification and lock screen still offered ±10 s and a seek bar, a headset's buttons still moved the position and the MiniPlayer kept its rewind; ### Fixed
- **Episodes hosted on Anchor / Spotify for Podcasters (and any other ffmpeg-made M4A) could not be transcribed** — the job died within a second of starting with "Extracted no audio", so the row fell back to the *Transcribe* pill and the alert said only "Could not transcribe this episode" (Bookshop Stories, episode 8, reported 2026-09-05 and reproduced on the emulator). Such files carry an MP4 edit list whose `media_time` is the AAC encoder delay (2048 samples = 46 ms); Android's MediaExtractor applies it as a timestamp shift and reports the first two frames at a *negative* sample time, and the native window reader in the sherpa-onnx patch (`AudioExtractor.kt`) took a negative sample time for end-of-stream and queued EOS before decoding a single frame. End-of-stream is now what it should always have been — `readSampleData` returning nothing — in both the windowed reader and the full-file path, and negative timestamps are clamped to 0 for the decoder. MP3 podcasts and M4B audiobooks without an edit list never hit this, which is why every earlier test passed.
- **Unsubscribing now removes everything.** It already deleted the downloaded audio and the rows (episodes, transcripts and their search index, the podcast), but it left the player running on one of the podcast's episodes — the MiniPlayer kept a ghost episode whose file had just been deleted — and the Player screen open on it; and no delete path (download removed, chapter, collection, podcast) ever cleared the episode's transcription resume marker in AsyncStorage. `episodeService.unsubscribePodcast` now does it all in one place: drops queued or running transcriptions *and* their markers (`whisperService.forgetTranscription`; a plain cancel keeps the marker so a retry resumes), tears the player down if it is on one of the episodes, deletes the files, deletes the rows, and broadcasts `unsubscribe` with the episode ids so the Player leaves. Deleting a download, a chapter or a collection forgets the marker too. Vocabulary words and lookup history saved from the episodes are deliberately kept — they are the learner's, not the podcast's.
- **Orphaned files are swept at launch** (`episodeService.sweepOrphanFiles`, before any screen can start a download): the `.part` of a download the process was killed in the middle of, an `episode_*.mp3` no row refers to any more (unsubscribing in releases before 2.3.0 deleted rows but not files), and an `imports/<id>` folder whose collection is gone. Downloads are matched by file name, so rows written by older builds with a differently spelled path are never mistaken for orphans; a failed database read skips the sweep rather than risk a live file.
- Imported chapters failed to transcribe with "Failed to instantiate extractor": expo-file-system URIs are percent-encoded (`001-Emily%20Henry.m4b`) and whisperService handed the native decoder the path with the scheme stripped but not decoded. `fileUriToPath()` decodes it (`4f4cb5b`).
- **Transcription failures now say why.** The alert used to read "Could not transcribe this episode. Make sure the AI model is downloaded in Settings." for every cause — a chapter Android cannot decode, a missing speech model and a corrupt download all looked the same, and there was nothing to report. `describeTranscriptionError` now names the case (*Speech Model Not Ready*, *Invalid Audio File*, *Audio Could Not Be Decoded*, *Transcription Stalled*, *Not Enough Storage*, or *Transcription Failed*), tailors the advice to imported files (which cannot be "re-downloaded": convert to MP3 / M4B and import again) and ends with a *Reason:* line carrying the underlying error, native decoder messages included. Model download and model load failures are named as such by whisperService (`MODEL_DOWNLOAD_FAILED`, `MODEL_INIT_FAILED`).
- The audio pre-flight check rejected every file whose first bytes it did not know — FLAC, Matroska / WebM, AMR, and MP4 files that do not open with `ftyp` — as "Invalid audio file" although Android decodes them. It now knows those signatures, and an unknown but clearly binary header is passed on to MediaExtractor (the authority) rather than refused; only a saved error page (HTML / JSON / text) or an all-zero file is still rejected up front, with the offending header quoted in the message.

- **Swipe-down closes the translation and word cards from anywhere on the card** — the drag gesture sat on a `Pressable`, whose own responder handlers silently replaced it, so only a tap outside the card ever closed it. Both cards now share `SheetModal` (drag on a plain `Animated.View`, backdrop as a sibling, body scroll only when content overflows).
- **Word pronunciation actually plays** — the dictionary's recording host answers HTTP 502 for most words, so the speaker button either never appeared or did nothing. Pronunciation now uses on-device text-to-speech (`expo-speech`, en-US), works offline for every word, and falls back to the recording only if TTS errors.
- **Mini player no longer parks over the tab bar** — when it hid while still mounted (episode ended, stopped from the notification, transition back from the Player) it only slid 120 px, leaving its top ~40 px over the tabs. The hidden offset is now computed from its measured height.

### Added
- **Settings › Learning › "Pause while looking up"** (default on) — playback pauses while a word or sentence card is open and resumes when it closes; a podcast that was already paused stays paused.
- **Copy / share the English text** from both cards, plus **"Ask ChatGPT, Gemini…"** when translation fails (shares a ready-made translate-and-explain prompt in your Settings language via the system share sheet); a compact "Ask an assistant" link is available on success too.

## [2.0.1] - 2026-08-19

### Added
- **New default transcription model: NVIDIA Parakeet TDT-CTC 110M** (int8, via sherpa-onnx `nemo_ctc`) — roughly 3x lower English word-error rate than Whisper Tiny at the same ~100 MB download, with native punctuation and capitalization. CTC frame timestamps drive the existing word-by-word highlighting (no attention export needed). Non-autoregressive decoding means the Whisper repetition loops on music/silence/ad reads structurally cannot happen. License: CC BY 4.0 (attribution shown in the Settings model picker).
- **Tarball model downloads** — `ensureSherpaModel` now supports models distributed as `.tar.bz2` release assets (the Parakeet int8 export has no per-file HF host): downloads the archive, extracts it with the sherpa-onnx native extractor, flattens the needed files into the model folder, and cleans up. Interruption-safe (rename-on-complete + re-extract without re-download).

### Changed
- **Whisper hallucination filter is now model-aware** — the repetition scrubber only runs for Whisper-type models; CTC engines (Parakeet, SenseVoice) cannot loop, so their transcripts pass through unfiltered and legitimate repeated words survive.
- **Wrapper patch extended** — `@siteed/sherpa-onnx.rn` patch now routes `nemo_ctc` through the windowed long-file path (`shouldChunkOfflineWhisper` + `isOfflineWhisperModel`); without this, a 90-minute episode would take the full-buffer path and OOM.
- Whisper Tiny remains available in Settings (smaller download); users who explicitly selected a model keep their choice.

## [1.0.4] - 2026-04-02

### Fixed
- **Transcription queue robustness** — complete rewrite of the processing loop:
  - Second and subsequent transcriptions no longer fail: native Whisper context is explicitly released and recreated between jobs.
  - App no longer crashes on re-entry while transcribing: `TranscriptionService` now uses `START_NOT_STICKY` with a null-intent guard, preventing two native Whisper instances from competing for the same resources.
  - After a transcription error the queue no longer gets permanently stuck: broken context is torn down before every retry.
  - Retry logic: each transcription is attempted up to 3 times (with context re-init between retries) before the queue advances.
- **App kill / force-stop recovery** — closing the app abruptly mid-transcription is now safe:
  - `onTaskRemoved` added to `TranscriptionService`: notification disappears when user swipes app from recents, process exits cleanly.
  - Interrupted items remain in AsyncStorage and are automatically re-queued on next launch.
- **Foreground service ANR fix** — next queue item is deferred to a new event-loop turn via `setTimeout`, preventing the Android 8+ ANR from rapid stop/start of the foreground service.
- **Episode list refreshes after restored-queue items complete** — transcript badge now appears without the user navigating away and back.
- **Partial transcript on cancel discarded** — whisper.rn resolves with partial segments when `stop()` is called; these are now detected via `_abortCurrent` flag and discarded so cancelled transcriptions never write incomplete data to SQLite.

### Added
- **Cancel queued/active transcriptions** — the "Queued" badge is now a tappable button (clock + ×) that removes the episode from the queue. The transcribing indicator is now a tappable red "Cancel" button that immediately aborts the running transcription.
- **Transcription timeout watchdog** — 45-minute watchdog that fires `stop()` if a native Whisper transcription hangs and never settles.
- **Reset transcription queue** — new "Troubleshooting" section in Settings with a "Reset transcription queue" button that aborts everything, clears AsyncStorage, releases the native context, and stops the foreground service.
